Perspective

Will AI take my job? Hard no.

Crystal thinks about her work in three buckets — and only one of them is fully automated.

AI handles fully

Reports, data pulls, trend scanning, formatting — the mechanical work that used to eat hours.

AI assists, humans review

Weekly leadership reports, analysis, benchmarking — AI drafts, Crystal and Mel validate.

Human only

Relationships, trust, community building, advocacy cultivation — the work that actually matters most.

peerbound

The Build

The Advocacy Copilot

An always-on custom GPT in ChatGPT Pro that ingests data from across Canva's tool stack and produces a leadership-ready report every Thursday.

Data sources

  • Guru — internal knowledge base
  • Gong — call intelligence
  • Google Files — docs & sheets
  • Canva Files — design assets
  • Peerbound — customer proof & advocacy data

Built with

  • ChatGPT Pro custom GPT
  • Manual data uploads weekly
  • Structured prompt engineering
  • Iterative refinement over months

Started as "I don't want to do spreadsheets manually." Now leadership depends on it.

The Stack

Self-built AI and purchased software coexist

Crystal's team uses three categories of tools — each with a distinct role. They complement, not compete.

Analyze

Gong

Surfaces insights from customer conversations — what are people actually saying?

Activate

Peerbound

Puts customer proof in the hands of every team — SDRs, sales, marketing. Teaches them to fish.

Augment

Custom GPTs

The advocacy copilot, weekly reports, and a Customer Proof Assistant built on Claude by a colleague.

SDRs use the Customer Proof Assistant (built on Claude) to surface proof from Guru. Sales tends to reach for the Peerbound Slack bot instead. Different tools for different workflows.

Takeaway

Start small. Test one thing.
Optimize from there.

Crystal's copilot didn't start as a strategic initiative. It started because she didn't want to wrangle spreadsheets. Now leadership depends on it every week.

1

Find your painful, repetitive task

The thing you dread every week. That's your starting point.

2

Build a rough first version

It won't be perfect. It doesn't have to be. Just start.

3

Keep the human in the loop

Review everything. Trust builds over time — let the tool earn it.
